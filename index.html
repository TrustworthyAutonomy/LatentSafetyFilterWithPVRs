Overview

Ensuring safety of vision-based control systems remains a major challenge for deployment in critical settings. Classical safety filters (e.g., CBFs and HJ reachability) do not scale well when the agent only observes high-dimensional images. This paper studies whether pre-trained vision representations (PVRs) can serve as effective perception backbones for vision-based safety filters. We use PVRs for latent failure classifiers, Hamilton–Jacobi (HJ) reachability-based value functions, and latent world models, and compare frozen, fine-tuned, and trained-from-scratch variants across multiple robotics environments.

Key takeaways

  1. A task-specific representation can match or surpass frozen PVRs on safety-control tasks.
  2. Fine-tuning PVRs during safety-filter training substantially improves collision avoidance.
  3. Safety filters with DINOv2 consistently perform well across tasks.
  4. If a PVR is well suited for world-model learning, dynamics lookahead can beat critic-only switching; otherwise a Q-function is preferable.
  5. PVR-based safety filters are light enough for real-world / edge deployment (≤19.3 ms, ≤611 MB).

What we evaluate

Five popular PVRs as perception backbones for safety-critical components:

DINOv2 (patch) DINOv2-CLS VC-1 R3M ResNet-50 Scratch / WM-R baselines

For each backbone we study three uses in latent space:

  1. Failure-set classifiers that separate safe vs. unsafe visual observations.
  2. HJ reachability safety filters trained with DDPG / DDQN in the latent representation.
  3. Latent world models (DINO-WM-style) used for dynamics-lookahead switching decisions.

Environments

ManiSkill

Unitree G1 places an apple in a bowl. Failure: hand contacts the bowl.

Dubins Car

2D navigation with obstacles under overhead RGB. Failure: collision.

CarGoal

Safety Gymnasium goal reaching with hazards under egocentric vision.

CARLA

Ego vehicle follows a lead car. Failure: vehicle–vehicle collision.

Closed-loop switching

At runtime the filter chooses between the nominal policy \(\pi_{\mathrm{nom}}\) and the safe policy \(\pi_{\mathrm{safe}}\):
  • Critic-only: evaluate \(Q(z_t, a_{\mathrm{nom}})\) and intervene if the margin falls below \(\tau\).
  • Dynamics lookahead: predict \(\hat{z}_{t+1}\) with the world model, then evaluate safety of the predicted next latent state.
Across tasks, fine-tuned PVR filters generally reduce violations more than Scratch / WM-R baselines while preserving task success.
