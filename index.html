Designing Latent Safety Filters using Pre-Trained Vision Models

  • Yuxuan Yang
  • Ahmad Hamzeh
  • Maxwell Astafyev

Paper arXiv

Overview

Ensuring safety of vision-based control systems remains a major challenge hindering their deployment in critical settings. Safety filters have gained increased interest as effective tools for ensuring the safety of classical control systems, but their applications in vision-based control settings have so far been limited. Pre-trained vision models (PVRs) have been shown to be effective perception backbones for control in various robotics domains. In this paper, we examine their effectiveness when used for designing vision-based safety filters. We use them as backbones for classifiers defining failure sets, for Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability-based safety filters, and for latent world models. We discuss the trade-offs between training from scratch, fine-tuning, and freezing the PVRs when training the models they are backbones for. We also evaluate whether one of the PVRs is superior across all tasks, whether learned world models or Q-functions are better for switching decisions to safe policies, and practical considerations for deploying these PVRs on resource-constrained devices.

Approach

We study PVRs as shared perception backbones across three safety-related components:
  1. Failure-set classifiers that recognize unsafe visual states.
  2. HJ reachability-based safety filters operating in latent representations.
  3. Latent world models used for safety-aware switching decisions.
Across these settings we compare training from scratch, fine-tuning, and freezing the PVR backbone, and analyze which design choices transfer best across tasks and hardware constraints.

Findings

The paper evaluates whether a single PVR backbone dominates across safety tasks, compares latent world models versus Q-functions for deciding when to switch to a safe policy, and discusses practical deployment on resource-constrained platforms.
